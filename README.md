# TS 82 CAD

A small AutoCAD-style drawing tool for TS 82, Exploring STEM through Geometry, at Everett Community College. It runs in the browser and works offline. Nothing a student draws leaves their computer: drawings stay in the browser and save as `.dxf` files.

**Open it:** https://stem-evcc.github.io/Geo-CAD/ (the capitals matter: Pages addresses follow the repo name exactly)

**Proof Builder:** https://stem-evcc.github.io/Geo-CAD/proofs.html. Students drag reason tiles into two-column proofs, like the laser-cut tiles from Weeks 7 and 8. It has the Week 8 packet proofs (Proof 1, Proof 2, Done early) plus angle and triangle practice: 13 proofs in all. **Check my proof** marks each row and ticks the proof checker's list; **Give me a hint** points at the first row to fix. A link can open one proof directly, for example `proofs.html#square-check`. When a statement tile goes into a row, the picture lights up what it names (blue for the left side, pink for the right, purple where they share), with a one-line caption; while dragging, it previews over each row, and a small copy of the picture follows along when the main one has scrolled away. The white bunny loafs while they work and flops when a proof holds. **Turning it in:** every proof a student finishes is saved in their browser; at the bottom of the page they type their name and press **Download my proofs (PDF)** to get one letter-size page per finished proof (their statements and reasons, the time, checks and hints used, and their sentence about a fake tile) to upload to Canvas. **Clear my saved proofs** wipes it on a shared computer. Like the CAD, it is one file (`proofs.html`) and nothing leaves the student's computer until they upload the PDF themselves.

**Saving: My drawings, Download, Upload.** Every change saves by itself in the browser, a moment later, under the student's name: the first visit asks **Who's drawing?**, and the name shows at the top right with the time of the last save. **My drawings** (the stacked pages at the top, the name, or the Drawings panel on Home and Build) lists them with a small picture: Open, Download, Rename, Make a copy, Delete, and **History**, which keeps an earlier version every few minutes (and at each Download), up to 30, to go back to. Another student on the same computer types their name and sees only their own drawings; **Show everyone's drawings** lets them copy someone else's (no rename or delete of other students' work), and drawings saved with no name can be opened and claimed. **Clear my drawings from this computer** is for the end of class on a shared computer. All of it stays in that browser (IndexedDB) and the name is never sent anywhere.

To keep a drawing or move it between computers, students use **Download** (a `.dxf` in Downloads, named like `Maria-Lopez-project.dxf`) and put it in their EvCC Google Drive (drive.google.com, New, File upload). **Upload** (or `Ctrl+O`, or dragging a `.dxf` onto the page) opens it again as a new drawing in the list. If a lab computer shows Google Drive in the Save box, **Save As** can save there directly. The drawing's `.dxf` carries each word's grouping as TS82 extended data, so uploaded text still picks as one word and engraves filled; laser files don't carry it. A drawing kept in the browser before this update moves into My drawings on the first visit. If the browser blocks storage (some private windows), the top bar says to Download instead.

**The bunny:** a bunny pops up in the bottom corner with a tip when a student is stuck (an error with a known fix, a paver slip, a minute with nothing happening) and flops when the plot is clean. Click it to send it away; **BUNNY** in the status bar turns it off.

**The Build tab (for the final project):** next to Home. Everything works by typed command too, as in AutoCAD.

- **Project sheet** (`PROJECT`, or NEW then Final project): one 12 x 12 in plywood sheet (304.8 mm) for the laser parts, and a dashed 4 x 4 in box (101.6 mm) for the one part cut on the router or waterjet. **Plot** asks which one: the sheet makes the `-LASER` file, the box makes the `-ROUTER` file (moved so the box's corner is at 0,0). The box's plot warns about sharp inside corners a round router bit can't cut.
- **Shapes:** `POLYGON` (`POL`), `ARC` (`A`, three points or center), `FILLET` (`F`: click a rectangle or polygon, Enter rounds every corner; or two lines; `R` sets the radius), `EXPLODE` (`X`). Rounded corners are true arcs in the polyline, so they save to DXF as arcs, offset correctly, and measure exactly.
- **Lines:** `PLINE` (`PL`: one connected shape, straight pieces and arcs; `A` for a smooth curve, `L` back to straight, `C` closes), `XLINE` (`XL`: endless construction line through two points, or `H`, `V`, `A`ngle, `B`isect, `O`ffset) and `RAY`. Construction lines always go on DRAWN, never plot, and act as edges for TRIM and EXTEND.
- **Edit:** `TRIM` now cuts polylines too (open or closed, arcs included), `EXTEND` (`EX`: click near an end; lines, arcs and open polylines grow to the next object), `JOIN` (`J`: lines and arcs that touch end to end become one polyline; a loop becomes a closed shape for OFFSET, AREA and 3D), and a **Linetype** list (`LT`): Continuous, Dashed, Hidden, Center. Linetypes save to the DXF linetype table; dashed lines on a laser layer plot as dashes, and Plot says so.
- **Dimensions:** `DIMLINEAR` (`DLI`, across or up by where the mouse goes, `H`/`V` to force one), `DIMALIGNED` (`DAL`), `DIMANGULAR` (`DAN`), `DIMRADIUS` (`DRA`), `DIMDIAMETER` (`DDI`), `DIM` to pick, and `DIMSCALE` for bigger text and arrows. They go on a new DIMS layer (cyan) that never plots, follow MOVE, ROTATE, MIRROR and SCALE (the number updates), and save to DXF as lines, filled arrowheads and text that AutoCAD shows as they are. Their definition rides along as TS82 extended data, so TS 82 CAD reopens them as dimensions.
- **Patterns:** `ARRAYRECT`, `ARRAYPOLAR` (`AR` asks which) and `SCALE` (`SC`, with Reference), each named on its tip bar as a transformation (slides, turns, a dilation).
- **Text** (`TEXT`): words become outlines (closed shapes), so the laser, router and waterjet follow them and the DXF looks the same on every computer. Seven fonts, previewed in the student's own words: Atkinson Hyperlegible Bold, Nunito Black, Arvo Bold, Stardos Stencil Bold (the middles of O, A, B stay on when cut out), Pacifico (joined script: a cut-out word is one piece), and two single-line Hershey fonts for fast scoring. Overlapping outlines are joined, so no laser line runs through the inside of a letter. Pick Score, Engrave or Cut out; the dialog warns when letters would fall apart. One click picks the whole word; `EXPLODE` splits it into letters. Spanish accents (á é í ó ú ñ ü ¿ ¡) are in every font.
- **Area** (`AREA`, also on Home): area and perimeter of a closed shape, or of corners clicked one by one.
- **Model in 3D** (`3D`): the bunny suggests Tinkercad and Onshape, with steps for bringing the drawing along, and **Save a 3D shape (SVG)** writes the red shapes filled, holes as holes, for Tinkercad's Import.

**Tool guide** (`TOOLS`, the tiles button at the top right, or https://stem-evcc.github.io/Geo-CAD/#tools): every button with a looping picture of how it works, what it does and the steps. **Print this page** gives an 8-page handout. Hovering over any button shows the same picture in its card.

## Turning on the site (once)

1. In this repo: **Settings → Pages**.
2. Under **Build and deployment**, Source: **Deploy from a branch**. Branch: **main**, folder **/ (root)**. Save.
3. After a minute or two the address above works. It's https, so Chrome and Edge show the real Save dialog.

## Updating it

Replace `index.html` with the newest `ts82-cad.html` (from the week's `4-ts82-cad/` folder in the TS 82 materials) and commit. The site updates in a minute or two. Students already on the page reload to get it.

`.nojekyll` tells GitHub Pages to serve the file as it is.
